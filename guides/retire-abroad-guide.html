Retiring abroad can stretch your savings and add sunshine to your days, but only if healthcare, residency and community are solid.

Priorities for retirees

  • Healthcare quality and access: good hospitals within 30–60 minutes, and insurance that covers you as you age.
  • Residency route: many countries offer retirement or passive-income visas with minimum-income requirements. Check official sources.
  • Cost and currency risk: a budget with a buffer for exchange-rate swings.
  • Climate: tolerance for heat and humidity often changes with age.
  • Access to family: direct flights home matter more than you think.

See our best places for retirees.

Money & pensions

Check how your pension and social-security benefits are paid and taxed abroad, whether benefits are indexed in your new country, and whether a tax treaty applies. Get advice from a cross-border financial planner.

Rent first, buy later

Rent for at least a full year, through every season, before you buy property. It's the single best way to avoid regret.

Community is longevity

Research on long-lived communities consistently points to social connection and purpose. Choose a place where you'll have both, such as volunteering, clubs, faith groups or family nearby. Read how to live well anywhere.
