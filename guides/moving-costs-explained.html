Moving prices vary enormously, and knowing what drives them is the best way to avoid overpaying. Use our moving cost estimator for a planning range, then compare real quotes.

What drives the price

  • Volume/weight: the number one factor. Every box you don't move is money saved.
  • Distance: local moves are usually billed hourly; long-distance moves by weight or volume plus distance.
  • Timing: summer, month-ends and weekends are peak times. Mid-month, mid-week moves in autumn or winter are often cheaper.
  • Access: stairs, long carries, elevators and parking restrictions add time.
  • Services: packing, crating, storage, vehicle shipping and specialty items (pianos, art).

Types of estimates

  • Non-binding: the final price can change based on the actual weight and services.
  • Binding: a fixed price for the listed inventory and services.
  • Binding not-to-exceed: you pay the lower of the estimate or the actual cost. This is usually the best deal for consumers.

Always get estimates in writing, ideally after an in-home or video survey. Be wary of quotes given over the phone without seeing your belongings.

Valuation & protection

In the US, interstate movers must offer two liability options: basic Released Value protection (included at no charge but limited to 60 cents per pound per item) and Full Value Protection (the mover is liable for replacement value, for a fee). Other countries differ, so ask what's covered and get it in writing.

10 ways to pay less

  1. Get at least three quotes (compare here).
  2. Declutter ruthlessly before the surveys.
  3. Be flexible on dates and ask for the cheapest day.
  4. Pack yourself and pay only for fragile-item packing.
  5. Collect free boxes from local shops.
  6. Consider a container or pod for long distances.
  7. Sell large, cheap-to-replace furniture.
  8. Ask your new employer about relocation assistance.
  9. Keep receipts, since some moving costs may be tax-relevant where you live.
  10. Book early for peak season.
