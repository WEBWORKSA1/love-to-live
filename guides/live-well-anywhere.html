Researchers studying "Blue Zones", regions with unusually many centenarians such as Okinawa (Japan), Sardinia (Italy), Nicoya (Costa Rica), Ikaria (Greece) and Loma Linda (California), found shared habits you can adopt in any city.

Move naturally

Instead of relying on the gym, build movement into daily life: walk to errands, garden and take the stairs. That's one reason walkable cities matter.

Have a purpose

Okinawans call it ikigai, and in Nicoya it's plan de vida: a reason to wake up in the morning. After a move, rebuild purpose quickly through work, volunteering or a craft.

Downshift daily

Every long-lived culture has a stress-shedding ritual, such as prayer, a nap, happy hour with friends or remembering ancestors. Schedule yours.

Eat mostly plants, stop at 80% full

Beans, greens, whole grains and nuts are staples. The Okinawan saying hara hachi bu means stopping when you're 80% full.

Belong & put loved ones first

Faith or community groups, close family ties and a supportive friend circle ("moai" in Okinawa) are common threads. When you choose a new home, choose your people too.
