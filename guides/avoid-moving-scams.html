Most movers are honest, but moving fraud is real, and it usually starts with a price that looks too good. Watch for these red flags.

The red flags

  1. A quote without seeing your stuff. Reputable movers survey in person or by video.
  2. A large cash deposit up front. Legitimate movers typically take little or nothing until delivery.
  3. No licence number. In the US, interstate movers must have a USDOT number, which you can check on FMCSA's registry.
  4. A price far below the other quotes. Lowballing is the classic set-up for a "hostage load".
  5. Vague or blank paperwork. Never sign a blank or incomplete document.
  6. No physical address or reviews. Or a company name that changes between calls.
  7. A rented, unmarked truck shows up on move day.
  8. New charges on move day that weren't in the estimate.
  9. Refusal to give written estimates or to explain valuation coverage.

Protect yourself

  • Compare several written estimates (get vetted quotes).
  • Check licences, complaint histories and recent reviews.
  • Pay by credit card for chargeback protection where possible.
  • Keep an inventory with photos, and keep valuables and documents with you.
  • In the US, interstate movers must give you the FMCSA booklet "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move". Read it.
