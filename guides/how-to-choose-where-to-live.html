Choosing a city is one of the highest-leverage decisions you'll make: it shapes your career, your relationships, your health and your bank balance. Here's a framework that turns gut feel into a decision you can defend.

1. Write down your non-negotiables

Start with constraints, not dreams. Examples: a maximum budget, a work-authorisation requirement, a direct flight to family, a good hospital for a specific condition, or a school type. Any city that fails a non-negotiable is out, however beautiful it looks on Instagram.

2. Weight what matters

List 6–10 factors — safety, cost, climate, jobs, nature, culture, community, commute — and give each a weight out of 5. Be honest: if you've never used a museum pass, culture probably isn't a 5. Our City Match quiz does this maths for you.

3. Do the money maths early

Compare the salary you'll actually earn with the cost of the life you actually want. A high salary in an expensive city can leave you with less disposable income than a moderate salary somewhere cheaper. Run both cities through the cost-of-living calculator.

Rule of thumb: target housing at or below 30% of take-home pay. Above 40%, everything else in life gets harder.

4. Shortlist three, compare two at a time

Big lists create paralysis. Keep a shortlist of three and compare pairs head-to-head (compare tool). Head-to-head choices are easier and reveal what you really value.

5. Test before you commit

Book a 1–4 week trial stay in a neighbourhood you'd really live in, not the tourist centre. Work a normal day, buy groceries, try the commute at rush hour and visit on a rainy Tuesday.

6. Check community & belonging

Loneliness is the hidden cost of moving. Look for clubs, faith or interest groups, expat or newcomer meetups, and coworking spaces before you move, and book something for your first week.

7. Decide, then commit for 12 months

Most people need 6–12 months to feel at home. Set a review date a year out and give the city a fair chance. When you're ready, compare moving quotes and follow the 90-day checklist.
